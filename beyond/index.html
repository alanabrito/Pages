Three homes, one design language.

Brazil, France, and the United States each left something specific in how I work. So did the things I do with my hands and in the water. The short version of why my product instincts look the way they do.

Brazil France United States Practices

01 — Geography

Where the instincts came from.

BR

Brazil — rhythm

Origin, family, maracatu. Collective energy, and the conviction that something made together beats something made perfectly alone. It's why I'd rather have a team arguing about the right problem than a tidy spec nobody believes in.

FR

France — craft

Engineering at Centrale, and years of living somewhere that treats restraint as a form of respect. Structure, rigour, and a permanent suspicion of anything decorative that isn't also doing work.

US

United States — momentum

Scale, experimentation, and the habit of building in public. Ship it, watch what happens, adjust. The counterweight that keeps the French half from polishing something forever.

02 — Off-screen

Practices that reset the eye.

Water, clay, and rhythm. The things that keep the work loose and stop me over-thinking a decision that wants to be made with the hands.

01

Water

Surf, scuba, wakeboard. Balance, stillness, and speed — three different arguments with the same ocean.

02

Clay & colour

Pottery, candles, watercolour. Slow, physical, forgiving of a first attempt. Good practice for anyone who spends the week in abstractions.

03

Maracatu

Rhythm learned in a group, where your part only makes sense next to everyone else's. The most useful thing I know about teams.
