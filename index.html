01 — Day job

PowerPoint: the fundamentals and what's next.

I hold both halves. The craft work that keeps the product excellent for everyone who opens it, and the Copilot direction deciding what it becomes. Neither one is allowed to starve the other.

A

Fundamentals

Performance, reliability, editing craft, and accessibility for a product used by an enormous and enormously varied audience. This is unglamorous, compounding work — and it's what people actually notice. A feature nobody asked for cannot buy back a product that feels slow or fights you.

B

Copilot

Driving where AI goes inside the product: what it should draft, rewrite, and restructure, how it grounds itself in the user's own material, and where it should stay out of the way. Scoping what the model can genuinely do well today rather than what demos well.

C

Holding the tension

The interesting part of the job. AI investment competes directly with fundamentals for the same engineers and the same quarter. My work is making that trade-off deliberately and defensibly, instead of letting whatever is loudest win.

03 — Approach

How I work.

Four convictions that survived contact with large products and small ones.

01

Fundamentals are the feature.

Every product has a loud roadmap and a quiet one. The quiet one — speed, reliability, the thousand small edges — is what decides whether people keep the product open. I protect that budget, especially when something newer is competing for it.

02

Quality is a measurement problem.

"It feels better" is not a ship signal, and it's a particularly bad one for AI. I define what good looks like before the work starts, build evaluation sets from real user material, and hold the bar when the schedule argues.

03

Accessibility is the sharpest lens.

I build screen-reader-first apps on my own time, and it changes how I spec everything else. If a feature only works when you can see it and aim precisely, it isn't finished — it's narrow. That test catches bad design long before an accessibility review does.

04

Direction beats output.

The senior version of this job is not shipping more. It's making sure a large group of capable people is pointed somewhere worth going, with a shared idea of what "done well" means — and then getting out of the way.
